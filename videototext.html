Academic research workflow

Collect consented ZSL samples.

A ZSL model needs real, labeled ZSL video examples. Record one sign at a time; clips download directly to your device and are not uploaded by this page.

Start the camera first. Use a pseudonymous participant code; do not include names or identifying details in labels.

Research safeguards: Obtain ethics approval and informed consent before collecting participants’ video. This page saves each clip and annotation CSV through your browser downloads; it does not transmit or permanently store samples. Keep recordings in an access-controlled location and follow your institution’s retention and deletion rules.

Separate local experiment

Test image classification with ASL digits.

This small downloaded dataset contains still photos of ASL digits 0–9. It is not ZSL video data and does not train or connect to this page’s ZSL camera recognizer.

For a local computer experiment, train and run the separate webcam demo from the project folder:

python scripts\asl_digits_demo.py train "C:\path\to\Sign-Language-Digits-Dataset-master.zip"
python scripts\asl_digits_demo.py camera

The experimental camera output is labeled ASL digits, not ZSL. The existing ZSL transcript stays separate and requires a genuine ZSL-specific model. See the README for requirements and limitations.

Optional sign photos

Look up recognized words.

Recognized text can be searched without changing the transcript. ZSL photos appear first, supplementary photos fill gaps, and unmatched words use ZSL fingerspelling.

Start a camera session with a recognition model, or enter text above to see sign photos.

Accuracy note: Hand tracking by itself cannot translate sign language. Sign recognition needs a language-specific model trained on appropriate data, including movement and context. This page does not guess signs from hand shape; without a configured model it still supports manual text entry and dictionary lookup.
